## CATEGORY 1 — SETUP & INSTALLATION
*[2:30 – 4:00]*
*[Scoreboard appears: Category 1 — Setup & Installation Reality]*

Let's start where every owner starts — getting the thing actually working.

All three are marketed as plug-and-play. All three have asterisks.

The EcoFlow Stream Ultra X weighs 38.8 kilograms. That's a two-person job just to move it. And in the UK — this matters — it legally needs to be hardwired into your distribution board by a certified electrician. You also need to notify your distribution network operator before you can export power to the grid. That is not plug-and-play. That is a professional installation with paperwork. Budget for it.

The Zendure Hyper 2000 is genuinely the most DIY-friendly of the three for a new build. Panels plug straight in, battery stacking is clean and modular. The friction doesn't show up in the hardware — it shows up in the app setup. We'll get to that.

The Anker Solarbank 2 Pro has been consistently praised for how straightforward the hardware setup is. Where it gets complicated is the smart meter — that requires some basic electrical knowledge, and a number of owners have reported the smart meter unit itself failing entirely and needing replacement, with slow support response when it does.

What all three share: none of them are quite as simple as the marketing suggests. The question is which kind of complexity you can live with.

## CATEGORY 2 — APP & SOFTWARE
*[4:00 – 5:45]*
*[Scoreboard: Category 2 — App & Software: The Daily Experience]*

This is the category with the most real owner data — because every single person who buys one of these uses the app every day.

Let's start with something all three have in common.

Every one of these systems ties its "smart" features to a cloud server. When the cloud goes down — or your internet drops — the unit keeps running, but the intelligent control stops. This isn't one brand's flaw. It's an industry-wide reality right now. They're all fighting the same battle. They just handle it differently.

EcoFlow's app is polished and detailed — good real-time monitoring. But there's a specific bug confirmed across multiple independent Reddit threads: the app reports solar generation at night, and shows impossible spikes in household demand that trigger unnecessary grid imports. Not a fringe report — a pattern. The AI energy management is described by owners as behaving more like a basic timer than genuine intelligence — it charges from the grid during cheap-rate hours, stops solar charging once the battery is full, and doesn't adapt the way the marketing implies. And some of the most-advertised smart features sit behind a paid subscription — starting around four euros a month, potentially much higher depending on how many devices you connect.

Zendure has the strongest local control of the three — MQTT, HTTP API, genuine Home Assistant integration, all there and praised by the power-user community. But the app has a recurring problem: it shows stale data while the unit is still actually working. Units regularly drop Wi-Fi and need a manual reset. And there's a specific repeated complaint from Home Assistant users — every firmware update breaks their automations. The engineering team appears responsive when bugs are reported directly to them. Getting to the engineering team is the problem.

Anker's app is genuinely praised for its clean interface and real-time monitoring. The frustration is cloud latency. The Solarbank 2 Pro requires Anker's cloud for its zero-export regulation — meaning the system is trying to match your household consumption in real time, but through a cloud loop. Small amounts of power bleed to the grid unnecessarily. Not catastrophic. But real, and documented consistently.

## CATEGORY 3 — REAL-WORLD PERFORMANCE OVER TIME
*[5:45 – 7:00]*
*[Scoreboard: Category 3 — Does It Actually Do What It Claimed?]*

This is where we move from what owners feel to what independent testers actually measured.

EcoFlow Stream Ultra X: round-trip efficiency drops to approximately 55 percent in cold conditions — around 12 degrees Celsius. The unit has a built-in self-heater that activates below five degrees, but the efficiency gap in cool weather is real and documented. For anyone running this system through a British or northern European winter — that's worth knowing before you buy.

Zendure: an independent professional reviewer measured 87 percent round-trip efficiency. Genuinely high. That's not marketing copy — that's tested. The same reviewer also measured operational noise at 39 decibels, against a claimed 25. That's not rounding. That's measurably louder than what was promised.

Anker: Notebookcheck measured approximately 13 percent energy loss during grid charging, and output dropping below two kilowatts during extended high-power operation. Specific. Tested. Documented.

The good news across all three: LFP battery chemistry and 10,000-cycle ratings are holding up in real use. The longevity concerns that exist tend to be firmware failures bricking units — not actual battery degradation.

## CATEGORY 5 — VALUE: WAS IT WORTH IT?
*[7:45 – 8:45]*
*[Scoreboard: Category 5 — Value Perception Over Time]*

This one gets personal.

EcoFlow owners on dynamic energy tariffs — Octopus in the UK, similar providers across Europe — report estimated savings of £600 to £900 a year, with payback around two years when the AI time-of-use scheduling is working well. That is a genuinely strong return. The caveat: that's on an optimised tariff, with the app behaving as intended. When the AI underperforms — and we've seen when it does — those numbers shrink.

Zendure owners who are technically confident — Home Assistant users, people comfortable with API integrations — tend to be very satisfied. The regrets cluster around hardware failures and long warranty wait times. One piece of advice repeated across multiple forums: track your actual energy consumption for a full year before adding a battery. You need to know exactly what you're gaining before you can judge whether the investment pays off.

Anker Solarbank 2 Pro: the community payback estimate is longer — four and a half to six years, depending on usage and tariff. The hardware is trusted. But owners of the Solarbank 2 and 3 have a specific grievance: the new Solarbank 4 Pro adds native local control that the previous generation isn't getting as a firmware update. People who bought in early feel they've been left behind.

## CATEGORY 6 — CUSTOMER SUPPORT
*[8:45 – 10:00]*
*[Scoreboard: Category 6 — Customer Support When Something Breaks]*

This is where brand character shows. And the research in this category is the most consistent of any we looked at.

EcoFlow has a clear pattern. Simple hardware failure — unit won't turn on — tends to get a fast replacement, sometimes within days. The community has documented this positively. But complex software issues enter a different process. Cases get escalated to a "senior team." Then weeks of silence. Then requests for videos and logs. Then: "we're working on a firmware update with no release date." The EcoFlow subreddit runs a monthly Product Issues Megathread — which tells you something about how frequently these issues arise. Community advice: buy from a retailer with a strong returns policy, not directly from EcoFlow. And support goes dark during Chinese public holidays — multiple users flagged this specifically.

Zendure's support is genuinely inconsistent — and that is the accurate version of this story. Some users get fast, helpful responses. Others describe being ghosted for weeks. The connector issue on the 2400 AC was handled case by case — no recall, no blanket fix. One finding worth noting: users who reported bugs directly to Zendure's development team sometimes got very fast resolutions. The engineering team appears responsive. Getting to them is the problem.

Anker SOLIX has the cleanest record of the three based on current data. Trustpilot scores are consistently higher. The main friction: returning a 30-kilogram battery as a hazardous goods shipment is a logistics headache, and replacement processing has been slow for some owners. But no pattern-level support failure story was found.

## CATEGORY 7 — SAFETY
*[10:00 – 11:00]*
*[Scoreboard: Category 7 — Safety Track Record — Weight ×3]*

We're going to be precise here. Because this is the category where being unfair does real damage.

A recall on one product does not mean a brand's whole range is unsafe. And no recall is not the same as no risk — it might just mean the product is new.

EcoFlow has had real, documented recalls. Multiple power station models — including the DELTA Max 2000, recalled in October 2025 for overheating and fire risk, resolved via firmware update. These are serious incidents taken seriously. But here is what the research confirmed clearly: **none of EcoFlow's recalls have touched their plug-in balcony battery products.** The Stream Ultra X, its predecessors — no safety incidents found. The recalls are real. The product category overlap is not.

Zendure: there is a documented connector overheating issue on the SolarFlow 2400 AC. Multiple confirmed cases on Zendure's own community forum, acknowledged by moderators as affecting a small percentage of units. Symptoms reported: burning smell, fused connectors, sudden power loss. Handled case by case. No recall. No evidence of the same issue on the Hyper 2000 or newer models.

Anker SOLIX: no safety incidents found on the Solarbank line. The 2025 power bank recalls — over a million units, fire and explosion hazard — involved consumer portable chargers. A completely different product line. The Solarbank uses LFP battery chemistry, which is inherently more thermally stable. And Anker's response to those power bank recalls — proactive, large-scale, publicly handled — is arguably a positive signal for how they'd manage any future Solarbank issue.

*[Beat]*

One honest note: the Solarbank 4 Pro and the Zendure Mix Pro are two to four months old as we publish this. That is exactly the window when early failure patterns start to surface. We'll be watching.

So. Which one?

Here's the honest answer: it depends entirely on who you are.

If you want maximum output and you're comfortable with professional installation — and you're on a dynamic energy tariff that lets the smart features actually earn their keep — the **EcoFlow Stream Ultra X** has the strongest potential return when everything works right.

If you're a technically confident buyer who wants genuine local control, real Home Assistant integration, and the flexibility to build and expand your own way — **Zendure**, specifically the Hyper 2000, is the pick. If you want something that just works, with the largest support community, the cleanest safety record on their plug-in products, and the most beginner-friendly experience — **Anker SOLIX Solarbank 2 Pro** is where most people in the European balcony solar community point newcomers.
