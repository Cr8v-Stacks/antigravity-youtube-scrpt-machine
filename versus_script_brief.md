## 1. The Core Concept

**"I did the research so you don't have to."**

We are not testing these products. We don't own them. We are doing deep, honest research — Reddit, brand forums, review platforms, independent professional reviews — and synthesising it into something useful for someone who is about to spend €1,500–€2,500 on a plug-in battery or solar system.

Think of it as investigative consumer journalism, not a hands-on review. The credibility comes from *how thoroughly and fairly* we sourced the information, not from claiming we used the products.

**Comparable reference:** the YouTuber who did "I tested EcoFlow's subscription so you don't have to" — same energy, but ours is research-led, not testing-led.

## 2. Product Scope (Non-Negotiable)

**IN SCOPE — plug-in solar and plug-in batteries only:**
- Plug-in batteries: units like Zendure SolarFlow series, EcoFlow Stream series, Anker Solarbank series — wall/solar-charged home storage that feeds back via a standard plug/outlet, designed for renters and balcony setups
- Plug-in solar: balcony solar systems — panel(s) + microinverter plugging into a standard socket

**OUT OF SCOPE — do not include, do not conflate:**
- Portable power stations (EcoFlow River/Delta, Jackery etc. used as standalone/camping units)
- Traditional rooftop solar + string inverter systems
- Whole-home batteries (Tesla Powerwall etc.)

**Primary comparison layer — recent models with real user data:**
Use whichever recent (not necessarily latest) model from each brand has the most real, usable owner feedback across Reddit, forums, and review platforms. This is determined by the research pass, not assumed upfront.

Even if data is thin on the new flagships, include them as a forward-looking mention. We are not criticising these brands — we are talking about them *because* of how popular and well-regarded they are. The flagships give the video freshness and relevance. Just be honest about evidence levels.

## 5. Scoring / Evidence System

Every scored point is tagged by the evidence tier behind it — shown on screen as it's scored:

| Tier | What it means | Visual |
|------|--------------|--------|
| **Tier 1** | Real owner/community reports — Reddit, brand forums, verified buyer reviews | ✅ |
| **Tier 2** | Independent professional testing with measured numbers | 🔬 |
| **Tier 3** | Manufacturer claim only — explicitly labeled unverified | ⚠️ |
| **Inconclusive** | Evidence exists but is too thin, conflicting, or skewed to score fairly | ❓ |

**Key rule:** When evidence is thin for a brand in a category, mark it **Inconclusive** — *not* zero. A zero implies a failed test. Inconclusive means we didn't find enough to judge fairly, which is an honest and neutral position.

**How to handle biased reviews:**
- Pattern over individual — one angry post = noise; same complaint from 12 people across platforms = signal
- Always name the source type: "multiple owners on Zendure's own forum reported..." not "people say..."
- Acknowledge review skew briefly in the script: unhappy owners post, happy ones mostly don't — this makes us sound credible, not defensive
- Never editorialize — report what was found, let the viewer weigh it

## 7. Tone & Editorial Stance

- **Fair, almost to a fault.** We are talking about these brands because they are doing great work in a space we care about. This is not a prosecution. We are not trying to make any brand look bad.
- **Curious investigator, not judge.** The posture is: we went deep so you don't have to. We're sharing what we found.
- **Honest about our limitations.** We don't own these. We didn't test them. Our credibility is in the sourcing and the fairness, not in false authority.
- **Neutral on thin evidence.** Don't fill evidence gaps with suspicion. Say it's thin and move on.

---

## 8. Framing & Structure Principles (Critical for Retention)

**The most important thing in this script is framing.**

- **Don't answer the core question too early.** If viewers feel they know the verdict by minute 2, they'll leave. The tension — which brand holds up best based on real people's experiences — has to stay alive all the way through.
- **Progress through categories with building stakes.** Each category reveal should feel like it's adding to a picture, not settling things prematurely.
- **Use specificity to create curiosity.** Not "some users complained about the app" — "one owner on Reddit described opening the app to find three months of energy data had vanished." Specific stories hook; vague summaries don't.
- **The "we did this so you don't have to" hook** should feel like a gift to the viewer, not a disclaimer. Lead with it as the value proposition, not the caveat.
- **New flagships = the exciting forward-look.** Bring them in near the end — here's what's coming, here's what we know so far, here's what to watch for. Gives the video a natural reason to stay current.
